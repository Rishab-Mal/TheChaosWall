What you're seeing

Both pendulums obey exactly the same deterministic physics — no randomness, no noise. The only difference is that Pendulum B starts with θ₁ larger by ε radians. Initially the tip separation is essentially ε. In a chaotic regime, this gap grows exponentially — roughly doubling every Lyapunov time (~1–2 s for a double pendulum at high energy).

The chart shows separation on a log₁₀ scale. Exponential growth appears as a straight rising line. The slope gives an estimate of the Lyapunov exponent λ — the rate at which nearby trajectories diverge. Once separation exceeds ~0.5 arm lengths, the two pendulums are fully decorrelated: the same tiny initial difference has produced completely different futures. Smaller ε just delays the inevitable.
